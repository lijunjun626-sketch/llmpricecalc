AI is changing
UK STEM jobs.

The useful question is not “Will AI take every job?” It is which tasks, entry routes, skills, and review responsibilities are changing—and what evidence supports each claim.

THE EVIDENCE IN ONE VIEW

AI is changing work design faster than it is providing a simple jobs-lost/jobs-gained story.

Several 2026 sources point in the same direction: demand for AI skills has increased, routine junior work can be affected, and workers spend meaningful time checking AI output. These are different measurements from different samples; they should not be added together or used to predict an individual person’s outcome.

UK JOB POSTINGS

AI-skilled roles rose

PwC reports 180,000 UK postings requiring specialist AI skills in 2025, up from 112,000 in 2024. That describes advertised demand, not a guarantee of an offer or a salary for every applicant.

STEM WORKFORCE

Junior pathways are under pressure

SThree’s 2026 survey found 32% of respondents said seniors now do work that used to belong to juniors. That is a warning about training routes—not proof that junior hiring has disappeared.

EARLY CAREERS

Fear is real; causation is harder

Prospects reported that many graduates worry AI reduces opportunities. Their research also cautions that wider economic conditions still shape job outcomes.

The evidence does not support panic headlines.

A survey about perceived job change cannot prove that AI caused every resignation or vacancy. Treat it as a signal to build skills and ask better questions—not as a personal career forecast.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY CHANGING

Four practical shifts for STEM workers and employers.

01 / TASKS

Routine work is less private

First drafts, basic coding, summarisation, and standard analysis increasingly happen with AI assistance. The human task shifts toward framing, checking, exception handling, and accountability.

02 / PROOF

A portfolio carries more weight

Showing a real project, the choices you made, and how you evaluated an AI output is more credible than claiming generic “AI proficiency.”

03 / REVIEW

Verification is part of the job

SThree reports that 46% of respondents spend more time verifying AI output. Faster generation is not automatically higher productivity if checking is poorly designed.

04 / HIRING

Skills combine rather than disappear

PwC’s barometer highlights demand for AI skills alongside creativity, judgement, and leadership. Domain expertise remains the thing that tells you whether an output is useful.

05 / TRAINING

Protect the apprenticeship tasks

Employers should identify the junior tasks that develop judgement. Automating every beginner task may weaken the future senior pipeline.

06 / LOCATION

Look beyond a single market signal

UK vacancy data, graduate outcomes, contract work, and regional demand move differently. Avoid treating one headline statistic as the entire labour market.

FAQ

Questions worth asking before making a career decision

Are AI jobs growing in the UK?

PwC reports growth in UK job postings requiring specialist AI skills. That is one useful indicator, but it does not describe all roles, all regions, or every applicant’s chance of being hired.

Is AI eliminating UK STEM entry-level roles?

Current evidence is more mixed than that. SThree highlights risk to junior development tasks, while its report says junior hiring was not down overall in its survey. Look at the actual role, employer, and skill pathway rather than a universal claim.

What should I learn first?

Start with a real domain problem, a relevant AI workflow, and a way to verify the output. A portfolio that demonstrates judgement is stronger than a tool certificate without evidence of application.
